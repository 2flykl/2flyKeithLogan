Use this as the **master Astra prompt**. It is written to frame the project as a stabilization and release-engineering task, not a redesign exercise.

> You are being given an existing browser game titled **BLACK & GIFTED**. Your job is to **audit, stabilize, repair, optimize, and prepare the game for release**.
>
> This is not a greenfield build. Do not redesign the concept, replace the game architecture unnecessarily, or remove working systems simply because they could be implemented differently. The game already has a strong visual identity, scene structure, character system, animation system, and narrative flow. The problem is that repeated revisions have introduced regressions, inconsistent asset handling, animation-scale problems, state-transition errors, and scene-specific bugs.
>
> Your role is to behave like a **senior gameplay engineer, animation-systems engineer, front-end game developer, technical artist, QA lead, and release engineer at the same time**.
>
> The primary objective is:
>
> **Take the existing build, understand how it currently works, identify why recurring bugs keep returning, repair the underlying causes instead of applying superficial patches, and deliver a stable release-ready version where all scenes, animation states, assets, videos, interactions, and transitions work reliably without breaking previously functioning systems.**
>

> ## 3. MOST IMPORTANT RELEASE PRINCIPLE
>
> The biggest recurring problem has been this:
>
> **A fix to one part of the game often breaks something that was already working elsewhere.**
>
> Therefore:
>
> **Regression prevention is the highest priority.**
>
> Before changing anything:
>
> 1. identify the current working behavior
> 2. identify the exact problem
> 3. isolate the affected system
> 4. change only what is necessary
> 5. verify that unrelated scenes and states still work
>
> Do not make global movement, scale, rendering, timing, or animation changes unless they are actually required.

> ## 4. CHARACTER SYSTEM — CRITICAL STABILIZATION
>
> The character system has repeatedly suffered from inconsistent scale and animation alignment.
>
> You must fully audit and normalize it.
>
> The male and female characters should behave as two variants of the same gameplay system.
>
> Their visual designs differ, but their gameplay logic should remain consistent.
>
> ### Fix these recurring problems:
>
> **A. Character scale inconsistency**
>
> The same character sometimes appears larger or smaller when transitioning between:
>
> - idle
> - walk
> - run
> - jump
> - uppercut
> - wall break
> - scepter walk
> - scepter run
> - scepter jump
>
> This must be normalized.
>
> Build or enforce a **single scale-reference system**.
>
> Every animation state should be calibrated against a common body-height reference.
>
> The player should not visibly grow or shrink merely because a different sprite sheet is being used.
>
> ---
>
> **B. Ground alignment**
>
> Different animation frames currently use inconsistent foot baselines.
>
> This causes:
>
> - floating
> - sinking
> - vertical jitter
> - bouncing
> - sliding
>
> Every sprite animation must use a consistent **feet anchor / ground anchor**.
>
> The feet should remain locked to the same gameplay ground position unless the player is intentionally airborne.
>
> ---
>
> **C. Walk cycle**
>
> The walk animation must visibly alternate limbs.
>
> It must not appear as:
>
> - gliding
> - heel clicking
> - shuffling
> - repeated same-leg-forward poses
>
> Verify frame chronology.
>
> If the source sheet contains inverse paired poses, make sure the playback order produces a true chronological gait.
>
> The final walk should visually read:
>
> left step → passing pose → right step → passing pose → repeat.
>
> ---
>
> **D. Run cycle**
>
> The run must also alternate arms and legs correctly.
>
> Eliminate repeated or near-identical poses that cause the character to appear like they are sliding.
>
> The run should have:
>
> - extension
> - recovery
> - alternating stride
> - consistent torso angle
> - consistent body height
> - consistent feet grounding
>
> ---
>
> **E. Jump cycle**
>
> Remove or avoid any jump frame that looks like the character is simply standing upright in the air.
>
> The jump should read as:
>
> crouch / launch → committed airborne pose → apex → descent → landing.
>
> Do not introduce an upright idle-looking airborne frame.
>
> ---
>
> **F. Uppercut**
>
> The uppercut can be slightly more dramatic and visually taller than the normal crowned state.
>
> However, it must still look like the same character.
>
> Do not scale it so much that the character suddenly appears like a different-sized person.
>
> Wall-break uppercut rendering must be independently calibrated from normal character-state rendering if necessary.
>
> ---
>
> **G. Scepter states**
>
> Audit all scepter states carefully.
>
> The male scepter jump has repeatedly been visually incorrect in scale.
>
> Normalize:
>
> - scepter idle
> - scepter walk
> - scepter run
> - scepter jump
>
> Make sure the scepter itself does not change scale or position erratically.
>
> ---
>
> **H. Male/Female toggle**
>
> The male/female toggle must remain fully functional across the entire game.
>
> It must not disappear after scene changes.
>
> It must not reset unexpectedly.
>
> The selected gender should persist unless the player changes it.
>
> Do not allow scene logic to overwrite the current character choice.
>

> ## 6. PANTHER SYSTEM — REBUILD CLEANLY
>
> Panthers are a major recurring source of errors.
>
> The intended panther behavior is simple and must stay simple:
>
> **State 1: Sitting**
>
> Panthers are initially seated / waiting at marked positions.
>
> **State 2: Running**
>
> When the player reaches or passes the trigger point, each panther transitions into a run and joins the player.
>
> There should not be unnecessary additional panther states unless required for a smooth transition.
>
> Remove or stop referencing **all old panther assets**.
>
> Use only the current approved assets:
>
> - sitting-state sheet
> - running-state sheet
>
> Make sure all previous panther images, fallback art, outdated sprite sheets, and legacy references are removed from active use.
>
> ### Panther behavior requirements:
>
> - Panthers are clearly sitting before activation.
> - They do not appear to already be running while stationary.
> - They transition naturally into running.
> - Their run state stays active during the chase.
> - Their scale is believable next to the player.
> - They remain grounded.
> - They do not flicker between old/new assets.
> - They do not spawn on top of the player.
> - Their timing should feel cinematic, not mechanical.
>
> Most importantly:
>
> **The player character must NOT automatically accelerate because the panthers are running.**
>
> The character should maintain the same movement rules and normal speed.
>
> Panthers should adapt to the player's movement, not force the player into a speed boost.
>
> Remove any hidden scene-specific multiplier or forced velocity increase tied to the panther sequence.
>

> ## 7. SCENE 1 — WALLS / MUSEUM / GIFT / SKY PROJECTION
>
> Scene 1 needs very deliberate cinematic sequencing.
>
> The correct sequence is:
>
> 1. The player begins uncrowned.
> 2. The first sky-memory projection is visible.
> 3. The player approaches the walls.
> 4. The walls fall / settle into the scene.
> 5. As the walls fall, the **first sky projection fades out**.
> 6. When the wall-fall sequence finishes, the sky should be clean.
> 7. The full museum should be visible with no projection covering it.
> 8. The museum should be allowed to visually breathe for a moment.
> 9. The gift box should appear slightly sooner than it currently does, during this full-museum showcase.
> 10. The player reaches the gift.
> 11. The player becomes crowned.
> 12. The first crowned uppercut / wall-break moment triggers the second sky projection.
> 13. That second projection can then remain associated with the wall-breaking phase.
>
> ### Important Scene 1 rules:
>
> The first projection should not abruptly disappear.
>
> It should **fade out during the wall-fall animation**.
>
> The museum should then briefly exist without video overlays.
>
> The gift should appear while the museum is fully visible.
>
> Do not make the gift appear too late.
>
> Do not make it appear before the museum reveal has completed.
>
> The timing should feel intentional:
>
> projection → falling walls → clean museum reveal → gift → crowning → second projection.
