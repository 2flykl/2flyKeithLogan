# Documentary information cards — editorial review

Reviewed 2026-09-28. Data: `js/site-africa-cinema-notes.js`.

## Evidence and limits

100 distinct cards: 50 visual observations and 50 national/contextual facts. The source registry contains direct primary-source links from the Government of Rwanda, Rwanda Development Board/Visit Rwanda, Rwanda Agriculture and Animal Resources Development Board, UNICEF, UNESCO and UN Tourism. Each contextual card carries its source in the player.

No transcript, subtitle file or embedded text track was available. This review used the actual ten Wix MP4s already linked in the theater, inspected visually at 5%, 20%, 40%, 60%, 80% and 95% of each video. It did not transcribe or translate speech, identify unnamed participants, or watch every intervening frame. Observations are grounded in sampled images; exact cut boundaries within each brief display window remain approximate. No dialogue, motives or cultural meanings are invented.

Context cards provide chapter-related national information, not evidence that a particular school receives UNICEF support, a particular gathering is Intore or Umuganda, a meal is a named dish, a plant is a particular crop, or a building is a named landmark. Unsupported ceremonial claims from the old Banana Crown note were removed. The Conclusion was verified as a montage, not treated as one continuous interview. The Music Video was verified as landscape footage, so its context concerns land and cultivation.

## Fact-check decisions

- Government overview: use stable geography, languages, elevation, capital, currency and time zone. Do not reuse its inconsistent/undated demographic figures or erroneous latitude prose.
- UNICEF education: national program context only; no identification of the filmed school or pupils' disability status. Accessible textbook work is explicitly dated 2021. The article was readable through indexed full text; direct extraction intermittently returned 404.
- UNESCO Intore: inscription is 2024; the precise dance in this film is not identified. The UNESCO page intermittently presents an anti-bot interstitial; indexed primary-source text was available.
- Visit Rwanda traditions: use documented craft practices and public-work descriptions. Do not infer that this documentary's work activity is Umuganda.
- RAB banana program: use research activities and collection locations, avoiding undated variety counts, yields or release claims.
- UN Tourism, *A Tour of African Gastronomy: Rwanda*, October 2024: food names and ingredients from pp. 3–5. Do not infer the filmed dish's identity.
- Visit Rwanda coffee/tea: use crop introductions and processing descriptions; omit undated employment/production percentages. Landscape images are not assigned a crop species.
- Heritage: museum and architectural context only; do not identify the thatched structure as King's Palace.

## Playback and presentation

Each note keeps the previous approximately 5.3-second on-screen interval and CSS fades. Visual/context pairs have a 1.1-second gap; longer rests between reviewed passages leave attention with the film instead of filling gaps with recycled facts. All cues use video time, not a repeating wall-clock loop. Pause and buffering freeze the current cue; seeking recomputes the cue directly, with no backlog. Rewinding intentionally revisits that passage's notes. Hidden panels are inert and absent from the accessibility tree. Focusing a source link pauses playback so it can be read without disappearing. Reduced-motion users get the same content and timing without animated fades.

Changing or replacing a video requires rechecking cue timestamps and this review log. Source URLs and exact card wording are kept together in the data file for future review.
